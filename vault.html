🏃 Vault

Power, speed, and precision. Intermediate to advanced vaulting — from front handspring to Yurchenko and beyond.

Intermediate

Intermediate Skills

Front Handspring Vault

Also known as: FHS vault, handspring vault

The gymnast goes over the table in a front handspring motion, landing on two feet. The foundation vault skill.

Before you try this
  • Front handspring on floor — automatic, no thinking
  • Squat-on and straddle-over vault mastered
  • Strong, fast run with consistent hurdle
Safety Tips
  • Use a vault trainer (raised stacked mat) for first attempts
  • Coach spots from the far side of the table
  • Thick landing mat is non-negotiable
  • Never attempt without a qualified spotter
  1. Build a fast, consistent run and powerful hurdle.
  2. From the board, push off with straight body and arms reaching to vault.
  3. Hands contact vault table simultaneously — push hard and fast.
  4. The block happens in under 0.1 seconds — practice on a wall first.
  5. Body rises over the table, hips over shoulders.
  6. Open and land with bent knees, arms forward, feet together.

Handspring with Post-Flight Salto

Also known as: FHS tuck, Tsukahara entry drill

A front handspring vault with an added tuck salto after the block. Develops post-flight awareness and height.

Before you try this
  • Excellent front handspring vault with full post-flight
  • Front tuck salto on floor — automatic
  • Strong block and high post-flight
Safety Tips
  • Land into a foam pit for all first attempts
  • Two spotters — one at table, one at landing
  • Must have consistent height in post-flight before adding salto
  1. Execute a powerful front handspring vault with maximum post-flight.
  2. As you rise off the table, immediately bring knees to chest.
  3. Tuck tightly and spot the mat below.
  4. Open tuck early enough to land safely — don't under-rotate.
  5. Land two-footed with bent knees and arms forward.
🔁

Yurchenko Entry Drills

Also known as: Round-off entry, Yurchenko prep

The round-off onto the board entry used in all Yurchenko-family vaults. Must be mastered before any Yurchenko vault.

Before you try this
  • Round-off back handspring on floor — automatic
  • Front handspring vault with good form
  • Round-off onto panel mat drills
Safety Tips
  • Drill round-off onto stacked mats before touching the vault
  • Coach must supervise all entry drill work
  • Wrist conditioning program required
  • Never rush this — wrists are vulnerable here
  1. Drill: round-off onto a springboard, landing two feet together.
  2. Progress: round-off onto board, back handspring onto a high stacked mat.
  3. Focus on a powerful round-off with feet landing simultaneously on board.
  4. The rebound must go backward — not sideways or piking down.
  5. Repeat until the entry is completely automatic before any vault contact.

Yurchenko Tuck

Also known as: Yurchenko, round-off entry vault

Round-off onto board, back handspring onto vault table, tuck salto in post-flight. One of gymnastics' most iconic vaults.

Before you try this
  • Yurchenko entry drills — fully automatic
  • Round-off BHS back tuck on floor — consistent
  • Coach and program director approval
Safety Tips
  • Foam pit landing required for all first full attempts
  • Two spotters recommended for initial attempts
  • Never rush from drills to full vault — progressions protect you
  • Stop if any wrist or shoulder discomfort occurs
  1. Run, hurdle, round-off with feet landing together on the board.
  2. Immediately rebound into a back handspring motion onto vault table.
  3. Hands contact table and block explosively in post-flight.
  4. Pull knees to chest in a tight tuck.
  5. Spot the mat and open for landing.
  6. Land on thick mat, feet together, knees bent.
🦸

Yurchenko Layout with Twist

Also known as: Yurchenko full, Yurchenko double twist

A Yurchenko vault with a layout (straight body) post-flight and one or more full twists. Elite-level skill.

Before you try this
  • Consistent Yurchenko tuck vault
  • Layout full twist on floor — automatic
  • Strong post-flight height
  • Elite program coach approval
Safety Tips
  • Foam pit required for all early layout/twist attempts
  • Spotter must be in place for every attempt until landing is consistent
  • Never attempt when fatigued — crashes are dangerous at this level
  1. Execute Yurchenko entry with maximum power and speed.
  2. Block off the table explosively for maximum post-flight height.
  3. Keep body long — do NOT tuck — arms drive the layout.
  4. Initiate twist by pulling one arm across body at the top.
  5. Keep body tight in layout throughout the twist.
  6. Open arms to decelerate twist, spot mat, land two-footed.
