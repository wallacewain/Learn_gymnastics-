💫 Floor

Tumbling, leaps, and artistry. Floor is where every gymnastics journey begins — master it here.

Beginner

Foundation Skills

🥚

Forward Roll

Also known as: Log roll, tuck roll

The very first tumbling skill. Teaches body awareness, rolling safety, and control.

Before you try this
  • No prerequisites — this is where gymnastics begins
Safety Tips
  • Always roll on a soft mat — never a hard floor
  • Tuck chin to chest — never roll onto your head/neck
  • Round your back like a ball — no flat back
  1. Crouch down, hands flat on mat shoulder-width apart.
  2. Tuck your chin firmly to your chest.
  3. Push with legs and place the back of your head on the mat — not your forehead.
  4. Roll along your rounded spine, keeping your tuck tight.
  5. Come up to standing without using your hands to push.
🔙

Backward Roll

Also known as: Back roll

Rolling backward safely — teaches the back-rolling motion used in many advanced skills.

Before you try this
  • Comfortable forward roll
  • Good body tuck awareness
Safety Tips
  • Hands go beside your ears — palms up — to protect neck
  • Never roll onto top of head or neck without hand support
  • Use an incline mat (wedge) to learn if needed
  1. Stand with arms in front, squat down and sit on mat.
  2. Roll backward on your rounded spine, bringing knees to chest.
  3. As hips go overhead, bring hands to mat beside your ears (palms face mat).
  4. Push with hands to lift neck and head over — don't let neck take weight.
  5. Continue over and push to standing, arms reaching forward.
🌉

Bridge (Back Bend)

Also known as: Crab, wheel pose

Back flexibility position essential for walkovers, handsprings, and beam work.

Before you try this
  • Shoulder and spine flexibility warm-up daily
  • Cobra stretch comfortable on floor
Safety Tips
  • Always warm up spine and shoulders before bridging
  • Never force or bounce into a bridge
  • Stop immediately if there is any wrist or back pain
  1. Lie on your back, knees bent, feet flat near hips.
  2. Place hands beside your ears, fingers pointing toward your feet.
  3. Push with both arms and legs simultaneously to lift into bridge.
  4. Straighten arms as much as possible, push hips high.
  5. Hold 5–10 seconds. Lower slowly — never collapse.
⭐

Cartwheel

Also known as: Side aerial (no hands, advanced)

The cartwheel is the gateway to almost all other floor and beam skills. Master this first.

Before you try this
  • Comfortable handstand (hold against wall)
  • Forward and backward rolls
Safety Tips
  • Practice on a soft mat, not hard flooring
  • Place hands in a straight line — not too wide apart
  • Don't collapse at the wrists — push through arms
  1. Stand sideways, arms overhead, step lunge onto lead foot.
  2. Reach first hand to mat, kick lead leg up. Hips rise over head.
  3. Second hand places on mat as first hand lifts. Keep arms straight.
  4. Legs pass through a split position overhead.
  5. Land one foot, then the other, finishing in a lunge facing opposite direction.
Intermediate

Building Skills

🌊

Round-Off

Also known as: Round-off, hurdle round-off

A cartwheel that lands facing backward on two feet. The most important tumbling skill — it connects runs to back tumbling.

Before you try this
  • Strong cartwheel in both directions
  • Good straight body jump
Safety Tips
  • Learn on a soft mat with a spotter
  • Don't pike down at the top — stay hollow
  • Land two feet simultaneously — not one at a time
  1. Run 3–4 steps, hurdle onto one foot.
  2. Kick into cartwheel but place both hands together on the mat.
  3. As legs come over, snap them down together (closing the body).
  4. Push off mat with both hands to add backward power.
  5. Land two-footed, facing the direction you came from, arms up.
🔥

Back Walkover

Also known as: Standing back walkover

An elegant backward acrobatic skill connecting bridge flexibility to back tumbling.

Before you try this
  • Bridge (full back bend) — comfortable and flexible
  • Bridge kickover with spotter
  • Splits on both sides
Safety Tips
  • Use a wedge mat or spotter for first attempts
  • Never force into arch if shoulders are tight
  • Stop if lower back or wrist pain occurs
  1. Stand tall, lift one leg and reach arms overhead.
  2. Arch back slowly, reaching for the mat behind you.
  3. Hands contact mat, one then the other, as the raised leg kicks over.
  4. Split through the air — front leg high, back leg following.
  5. Land in a lunge, then bring feet together.
💥

Back Handspring

Also known as: BHS, flic-flac, flip-flop

The cornerstone of back tumbling. Once you have a consistent BHS, advanced tumbling opens up completely.

Before you try this
  • Strong round-off
  • Comfortable back walkover
  • Bridge with good shoulder flexibility
  • Spotter-assisted standing BHS with good form
Safety Tips
  • Always spotted for first 50+ attempts — this is non-negotiable
  • Use a strap belt (overhead harness) in a proper gym
  • Never kick up — jump back and then arch
  • Wrists must be conditioned before starting
  1. Stand with arms by sides, bend knees slightly (dip).
  2. Swing arms and jump backward and upward — not straight back.
  3. Arch body as you leave ground, arms reaching overhead.
  4. Hands contact mat simultaneously — push FAST.
  5. Snap feet over quickly, landing with bent knees and arms up.
Advanced

Advanced Skills

🌪️

Back Tuck (Standing & Running)

Also known as: Back salto, tuck back

A backward somersault in a tuck position. Often the first salto skill gymnasts learn.

Before you try this
  • Consistent back handspring
  • Round-off back handspring with power
  • Tuck jump with good height and form
  • Harness/pit training with coach sign-off
Safety Tips
  • Foam pit only for first attempts — always
  • Overhead harness rig strongly recommended
  • Trained coach must spot for every early attempt
  • Do not attempt if mentally blocked — fear leads to injury
  1. Standing back tuck: dip, swing arms up, jump straight UP (not back).
  2. At peak height, pull knees tight to chest and grab shins.
  3. Tuck chin slightly — don't throw your head back.
  4. Spot the floor as you rotate around — open when you see the mat.
  5. Land two-footed with bent knees. Stick!
  6. Running version: add round-off for power, then punch off two feet into tuck.
🦅

Layout (Back Layout Salto)

Also known as: Full-out, back layout, whip

A backward salto in a straight (layout) body position. More height and power required than a back tuck.

Before you try this
  • Consistent round-off back tuck
  • Round-off BHS back tuck — powerful and clean
  • Good body tension and hollow position
  • Harness/pit training mandatory
Safety Tips
  • Foam pit required for early attempts — no exceptions
  • Overhead harness training is the standard path
  • Never rush from tuck to layout — the progressions exist for a reason
  1. Run, round-off, back handspring — building maximum power.
  2. Punch off two feet, driving arms up aggressively.
  3. Keep body long and tight — do NOT tuck.
  4. Head neutral, arms by sides or overhead.
  5. Rotate in a hollow body position.
  6. Open eyes, spot floor, land with bent knees.
🌟

Full Twist (Layout Full)

Also known as: Full, layout with full twist

A layout salto with a 360° twist. One of the most iconic and beautiful gymnastics skills.

Before you try this
  • Consistent round-off BHS layout
  • Half-twist (half full) in pit
  • Strong aerial body tension
  • Coach and program director approval
Safety Tips
  • Foam pit and overhead harness required for all initial attempts
  • Initiate twist from the shoulders — don't throw head
  • Never try a full twist when fatigued
  1. Execute a powerful round-off BHS into the punch.
  2. Jump and initiate layout rotation.
  3. At the top of the flip, pull one arm across your body to initiate the twist.
  4. Keep body long — twisting in a tight layout position.
  5. Spot the floor as you come around the twist.
  6. Open arms to slow rotation, land two-footed and stick.
