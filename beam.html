🤸‍♀️ Beam

4 inches wide, 4 feet off the ground. Build confidence step by step — from the floor line to full height.

Beginner

Foundation Skills

🦶

Walking on the Beam

Also known as: Beam walk, relevé walk

The very first beam skill. Learn to balance and walk confidently before any acrobatics.

Before you try this
  • Practice walking on a floor line (tape on floor) first
Safety Tips
  • Start on a low or floor-level beam
  • Have a coach walk beside you the first few times
  • Mats on both sides of the beam at all times
  1. Mount the beam from the side using a step-up or jump-to-support.
  2. Stand tall — eyes forward, not looking at your feet.
  3. Walk placing one foot directly in front of the other, toes pointed.
  4. Arms out to sides for balance — like a tightrope walker.
  5. Practice on floor line → low beam → full-height beam with mats.
⚖️

Relevé Balance

Also known as: Tiptoe hold, balance pose

Balancing on the balls of your feet on the beam. Builds ankle strength and core stability.

Before you try this
  • Comfortable beam walk
  • Can relevé balance on floor for 10 seconds
Safety Tips
  • Spotter stands beside with hand near your waist
  • Step off to the side if you lose balance — never pitch forward
  • Low beam first, always
  1. Stand in the middle of the beam, feet together.
  2. Rise slowly onto the balls of your feet (relevé).
  3. Arms extend to sides or overhead — whichever feels balanced.
  4. Eyes fixed on a spot ahead — don't look down.
  5. Hold 3 seconds → 5 seconds → 10 seconds as you improve.
🏅

Tuck Jump Dismount

Also known as: Jump off, basic dismount

A safe, confident way to get off the beam. Landing safely is its own important skill.

Before you try this
  • Confident beam walk and standing balance
  • Good jump landing on floor (stick landing)
Safety Tips
  • Always land on a thick mat — never on bare floor
  • Bend knees on landing to absorb force
  • Spot at beam height the first attempts
  1. Stand at the end of the beam facing the dismount direction.
  2. Swing arms and jump upward, bringing knees to chest.
  3. Open legs to landing position just before contact.
  4. Land with feet together, knees bent, arms forward for balance.
  5. Stick the landing — don't take extra steps.
🌟

Cartwheel on Beam

Also known as: One-arm cartwheel (later progression)

The first acrobatic skill on beam. Confidence here unlocks the whole intermediate level.

Before you try this
  • Perfect cartwheel on floor — 20+ consistent
  • Cartwheel on floor line (tape)
  • Cartwheel on low beam with spotter
Safety Tips
  • Spotter must hold wrist and waist until fully confident
  • Low beam with thick mats on every side
  • Hands go on beam one at a time — don't grab with both
  1. Practice on a floor line until it's automatic — no thinking required.
  2. Move to a low beam. Walk spotter hands the first 10 times.
  3. Kick up into the cartwheel, hands one at a time on the center of the beam.
  4. Look at the beam as your hands contact it.
  5. Finish with both feet landing on beam, one at a time, arms reaching forward.
Intermediate

Building Skills

🔄

Back Walkover

Also known as: Back walkover on beam

An elegant backward acrobatic skill that requires bridge flexibility and confident balance.

Before you try this
  • Consistent back walkover on floor
  • Cartwheel on high beam without spotter
  • Bridge kickover on floor
Safety Tips
  • Always spotted for first 30+ attempts on beam
  • Low beam with mats before full-height beam
  • Never do if back/shoulder pain exists
  1. Stand on one foot, lift the other leg to a high relevé.
  2. Reach arms back overhead and arch gently into a back bend.
  3. Place hands on the beam one at a time.
  4. Kick the back leg up and over, splitting legs as you go.
  5. Land one foot, then the second, finishing in a lunge.
🏃

Split Leap

Also known as: Grand jeté, switch leap

A leap with legs at or near 180° split in the air. The signature beam dance element.

Before you try this
  • Flat splits on both sides on floor
  • Split leap on floor with 180° separation
  • Comfortable running on beam
Safety Tips
  • Spotter alongside for first attempts on high beam
  • Practice landing on beam without the leap first
  • Never force splits — warm up thoroughly before every session
  1. Build momentum with 2–3 run steps on beam.
  2. Hurdle onto one foot and swing the back leg forward explosively.
  3. Jump up off the front foot as the back leg reaches max height.
  4. Reach both legs to full split in the air.
  5. Land on the front foot with bent knee, then bring feet together.
Advanced

Advanced Skills

🌪️

Back Tuck (Back Salto)

Also known as: Back tuck dismount, standing back tuck on beam

A backward somersault performed on the beam. One of the most crowd-pleasing beam skills.

Before you try this
  • Excellent standing back tuck on floor (30+ consistent)
  • Back tuck on a line on floor
  • Back walkover on full-height beam without spotter
  • Coach sign-off required
Safety Tips
  • Foam pit attempts only to start — no exceptions
  • Spotter required for every attempt until 100% consistent
  • Only attempt if back tuck on floor is truly automatic
  • Do not attempt if you are fatigued or nervous — come back
  1. Stand near center of beam, feet together, arms by sides.
  2. Swing arms up and bend knees for a small dip (countermovement).
  3. Jump straight up — not backward — arms lifting overhead.
  4. Pull knees to chest tightly and tuck chin slightly.
  5. Spot the beam as you rotate and open for landing.
  6. Land both feet on beam simultaneously with bent knees.
✨

Back Handspring Series

Also known as: BHS, flic-flac on beam

A back handspring performed on beam, connecting into a series. Requires exceptional power and precision.

Before you try this
  • Perfect back walkover on high beam without spotter
  • Back handspring on floor on a balance beam-width line
  • Back handspring on low beam with spotter
  • Coach approval — this is a high-risk skill
Safety Tips
  • Spotted every attempt until absolutely automatic
  • Use beam pads (cushioned beam) for early training
  • Never attempt on a wet or slippery beam
  • Have crash mats surrounding full-height beam
  1. Start with a consistent back walkover — this teaches the hand placement.
  2. From a stand, reach back and jump, pushing hips over head quickly.
  3. Hands hit beam simultaneously, centered and firm.
  4. Snap feet over and land together on the beam.
  5. Immediately connect to the next skill or hold for a stick.
